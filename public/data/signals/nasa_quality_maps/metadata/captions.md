# Map Captions — Swiss National Park ECOSTRESS

## map_01_dominant_daytime

**Swiss National Park — Ecological Signal Mosaic**

Dominant daytime ECOSTRESS signal classes reveal terrain-structured spatial
organization across the Swiss National Park. Each pixel represents the
dominant ecological signal class derived from multi-year June composites of
NDVI, ET, ESI, PET, and WUE. Hillshade base from NASADEM 30 m DEM, resampled
to ECOSTRESS 70 m grid. Classes are mutually exclusive; nodata pixels are
transparent.

*Data: ECOSTRESS + NASADEM · Swiss National Park · Daytime June analysis ·
Exploratory monitoring map*

## map_02_demand_efficiency_watch

**Demand-Efficiency Watch Zones**

Localized areas where atmospheric demand and vegetation efficiency signals
require closer monitoring. These pixels show positive NDVI and ET trends
alongside negative WUE trends, suggesting vegetation activating under
increasing atmospheric demand with declining water-use efficiency.
All other valid pixels are dimmed for visual clarity.

*Data: ECOSTRESS + NASADEM · Swiss National Park · Daytime June analysis ·
Exploratory monitoring map*

## map_03_esi_watch_zone

**ESI Watch Zones**

Areas where evaporative stress signals suggest possible terrain-linked
sensitivity. ESI watch pixels show positive NDVI and ET slopes alongside a
negative ESI slope — a pattern that may indicate localized surface moisture
limitation despite apparent vegetation greening.

*Data: ECOSTRESS + NASADEM · Swiss National Park · Daytime June analysis ·
Exploratory monitoring map*

## map_04_stable_core

**Stable Ecological Core**

Small but important areas showing consistent low-stress ecological signal
behavior across multiple years. These pixels display low interannual
variability in NDVI, ET, and ESI — a signal associated with stable, mature
vegetation communities likely anchored in favorable topographic positions.

*Data: ECOSTRESS + NASADEM · Swiss National Park · Daytime June analysis ·

## map_05_timebin_comparison

**Time-Bin Validation of Signal Classes**

Class structure remains interpretable across temporal filtering choices.
Four panels show the dominant ecological class derived from progressively
broader time-bin selections: midday only, daytime, daytime + evening, and
all available acquisition times. Core spatial patterns persist across bins,
supporting the robustness of the classification framework.

*Data: ECOSTRESS + NASADEM · Swiss National Park · June multi-year analysis ·
Exploratory monitoring map*

## map_06_terrain_context

**Terrain Context of Ecological Signal Classes**

Class structure is interpreted against elevation and topographic gradients.
Left panel: dominant daytime class over hillshade. Right panel: terrain field
(elevation or slope) over the same hillshade base. The visual alignment
between class boundaries and topographic zones supports a terrain-structured
interpretation of the signal mosaic.
